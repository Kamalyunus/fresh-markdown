Reading the backtest · Demand vs. sales

Why sales come in under demand

The report shows two numbers for every hour: the units customers wanted, and the units we actually sold. They are never the same, and the gap is not a modelling quirk — it is sales we lost because the shelf ran out. On a thin shelf that gap is large. Stock deeper and it closes.

Demand does not arrive one unit at a time

This is the part that surprises people. An item that averages one unit an hour does not sell one unit an hour. It sells nothing for most of the hour, then several at once. Half of all hours are completely quiet — and one hour in eight wants three units or more.

Same demand, same price — only the shelf changes

Each bar below is the same item at the same price, wanting the same one unit an hour. The only thing that changes is how many units are sitting on the shelf. The green is what we hand over; the orange is demand that walked out.

A bakery selling one croissant an hour.

Most hours nothing sells. Occasionally someone wants four at once. Stock two and you sell two — the other two units were wanted and you had nothing to hand over. Stock ten and you sell all four, and the ones wanted after that.

The demand forecast never changed. What changed is how much of it you could physically hand over.

Why this matters for markdown decisions

This is not a caveat on the numbers — it is the reason the pricing engine behaves the way it does, and the strongest argument for letting it.

Nearly empty shelf

A deeper markdown creates more demand, but there is nothing left to sell it. You give away margin on the units that were going to sell anyway. This is when holding price is right.

Well-stocked shelf

Extra demand can actually be served, and every unit that does not move becomes waste at full cost. This is when marking down pays — and the engine goes deeper.

It is also why the engine opens shallower than the old markdown ramp and still ends up losing substantially less: much of the old loss was discount given away on stock that was going to clear regardless.
