Perishable Markdown MVP · production integration

What gets logged every hour, and who has to produce it

The system writes two event streams: a decision event when it sets a price, and an outcome event when that hour closes. The decision event is written by the pricing service and needs nothing from you beyond the state you already send to ask for a price. The outcome event is the real integration ask — nine required fields per priced hour, one per decision, with inventory that reconciles or says why it doesn't. Every field below carries a real example value, and §05 walks one episode end to end in payloads captured from the running system.

01The shape of it

One SKU in one fulfilment centre inside a clearance window is an episode. Every hour of that episode is a decision interval: the service is asked for a price, it logs what it decided, the price goes live, the hour ends, and you report what happened.

Both streams are append-only JSONL with durable writes and duplicate detection. Nothing is ever silently dropped — an event that fails validation is written to a quarantine file with the reason attached, and the quarantine count is a monitored number.

You Price request 11 fields of state: what the item is, how much is left, how long it has, what price is in force now.
Service Decision event 36 fields. The price it chose, everything it chose from, and every input it used — enough to re-solve the decision later.
You Price goes live The applied price must be the one the service returned, or the mismatch rate climbs.
You Outcome event 9 fields when the hour closes: units sold, inventory in and out, the price that was actually in force.

Why the decision event is so wide

It is an audit trail, not a log line. pipeline.assurance takes a logged decision, re-runs the solver from the event's own fields, and checks it gets the same price back. That is how a drifted model artifact or a bad config deploy is caught in production — but it only works if the event carries the full demand forecast (mu_ref_path) and the price already in force (anchor_discount), not just this hour's summary. None of those 36 fields is yours to produce.

The service refuses rather than returning an unsafe price

If the state is inconsistent — negative inventory, cost above list, a horizon that disagrees with itself, no feasible tier at or below the current price — the call raises StateRejected and no price comes back. Your integration needs a defined fallback for that path. It is deliberately not a silent default.

The one that will bite: episode_close_write_off

When a clearance window closes, the source system writes off whatever is left and reports ending_inventory = 0 — even though stock remained. That is not a reconciling outcome, so it needs adjustment_reason set or it quarantines. It happens on about half of all episodes. An integration that omits it quarantines roughly half its final-hour outcomes and fails the event-completeness gate for what will look like a pipeline defect on our side.

The two legitimate values, and how to decide between them, given leftover = max(starting − sold, 0):

  • intraday_restock — ending > leftover. Stock was added mid-window.
  • episode_close_write_off — ending == 0 while leftover > 0.

Recognise the write-off by the zero, never by position in the episode. The source zeroes at its own window boundary; once a window is merged across midnight that row sits in the middle of ours. Two earlier implementations keyed it to "the last hour" and quarantined real outcomes in bulk.

A partial shortfall — 0 < ending < leftover — matches neither, and is left undocumented on purpose so that it quarantines. That is unexplained inventory loss, and the quarantine file is the only place it is visible. Please don't add a catch-all reason to drive the count to zero.

There is no exploration rate — plan for a lot of it

All three hours above explored, and that is not the seed being unlucky. The rule is simply: compute what every alternative price would cost in expected IL, keep the ones costing no more than tau_current, and if that set is non-empty, draw from it uniformly. There is no probability schedule, no base rate, no cap.

So a large share of priced hours will carry a price that is not the recommended one, and applied_price will differ from optimal_price on all of them. That is the product working, not a bug in the integration — but it is worth knowing before someone builds an alert on the divergence. The budget tau_current is what controls the volume, and it is recalibrated daily from realised spend.

What the third one costs if you get it wrong

Drop adjustment_reason from that last payload and the store writes it to quarantine with "ending_inventory does not reconcile and no adjustment_reason documented". It never reaches learning, event completeness falls, and — because this shape occurs at the close of roughly half of all episodes — the shadow gate will not pass.

Note also that is_stockout is false on all three, including the write-off. Stockout means demand hit the inventory ceiling, which is sold ≥ starting. Stock being written off at the close is a different event and must not be reported as a stockout.

06The thresholds these feed

Event quality is gated, and two of these gates can suspend the system. Worth knowing before you scope, because they set the reliability bar on the outcome stream.

GateThresholdWhat breaches it
event completeness≥ 99%Outcomes landing per decision. Quarantined outcomes don't land, so a missing adjustment_reason shows up here first.
matched decision rate≥ 99%Decisions that got an outcome back.
duplicate or unmatched≤ 1%Repeated ids plus outcomes with no matching decision, over finalized outcomes.
price mismatch≤ 1%Outcomes whose applied_price differs from the decision's by more than 1e-6. This is the "did our price actually go live" check.
missing stockout fieldexactly 0Any outcome at all without is_stockout.

Exactly-once, on our side

Learning consumes each outcome_id once and records that it did, so a retry that re-sends an outcome is safe: it is counted as a duplicate and ignored. You do not need exactly-once delivery — you need stable ids. Re-sending the same outcome under a fresh outcome_id is the failure mode to avoid, because that is indistinguishable from a second real observation.

07The parts worth checking first

Ranked by how much trouble they have caused in replay and shadow. If your team can answer these six, the rest of the contract is routine field plumbing.

  1. hard Can you emit the episode-close write-off?

    About half of episodes end with the source zeroing inventory. If that reason can't be produced, half the outcome stream quarantines and the shadow gate never passes. This is the single most likely blocker.

  2. hard Is episode_id stable across midnight?

    An episode is a maximal run of consecutive hourly rows for one SKU × FC where the window counter ticks down one per elapsed hour. Thirty-six-hour windows are common. If your episode key is derived from a date, it will split one episode into two and every horizon in it will be wrong.

  3. hard Does hours_remaining mean hours, reliably?

    It has to equal the number of hours actually left in the window, this one included. The service plans over exactly that many hours and rejects the state if its forecast path and your counter disagree. Upstream data has historically carried very large values here from defects; anything above 120 hours is treated as bad data, not a long window.

  4. medium Can you guarantee one finalized outcome per priced hour, including zero-sale hours?

    Roughly three quarters of hours sell nothing. Those are the observations that identify demand at a price, so a pipeline that only reports sales silently discards most of the evidence and completeness collapses.

  5. medium Is the applied price observable after the fact?

    The outcome must carry the price that was genuinely in force, which is not always the one we returned — a failed push, a manual override, a promotion stacked downstream. Reporting our recommendation back to us hides exactly the discrepancy this field exists to surface.

  6. medium Are cost and original_price available per hour, per FC?

    Cost sets the price floor, so a stale or missing cost either blocks legal discounts or permits below-cost ones. Both are per-request inputs, not reference data we hold.

Field lists are read directly from the running system — events/store.py (DECISION_REQUIRED, OUTCOME_REQUIRED), inference/decide.py for what the service emits, pipeline/shadow.py for the reference outcome producer, and config.yaml for every threshold quoted in §06. The shadow harness is a working implementation of the outcome side, including adjustment_reason, and is worth reading before writing a new one.
