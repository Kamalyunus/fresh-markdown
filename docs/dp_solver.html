STEP 1 · feasible_tiers

17 legal, 5 choosable

Discounts run from 0 in 2.5 pp steps up to the cost floor d_max = 1 − cost/price = 40%: 17 legal tiers. Nothing below cost is ever representable — the floor lives in the action space itself, not in a check that could be skipped. But mid-episode, everything shallower than the 30% anchor is history: markdown never retreats, so only the 5 tiers from 30% to 40% are choosable this hour. The shallow tiers stay in the value function (the same solver serves the entry decision, where they are reachable), but they cannot be picked here.

STEP 2 · demand at every (hour, tier)

Turn each price into a demand distribution

For every remaining hour and every tier, expected demand is the baseline scaled by the price ratio raised to the elasticity: mu = mu_ref × ((1−d)/(1−d_ref))^ε, with mu_ref the model’s prediction at the 25% reference. Then a full negative-binomial distribution over sold counts is built with dispersion r — the solver never works with averages, always with the whole distribution.

STEP 3 · the terminal condition

Price the end of the window

When the clock hits zero, every unit still on the shelf is scrapped at cost: V(h=0, q) = −cost × q. Two leftover units here cost −₩12,000. This single line is where all the urgency comes from — every decision above it is a trade between giving away margin now and eating that scrap later.

STEP 4 · backward induction

Fold the future back, hour by hour

Starting from the last hour and walking backwards, the solver scores every (tier, inventory) pair:

Q[j][q] = Σₖ P(sell k | mu, r) × [ min(k, q) · (−P0·d_j) + V[t+1][j][q − min(k,q)] ] └ censoring ┘ └ margin given ┘ └ the future, anchored at j ┘ V[t][a] = max over j ≥ a of Q[j][q] ← markdown can deepen, never retreat

Two details carry the design. First, min(k, q) puts censoring inside the recursion: demand beyond stock earns nothing, so inventory level changes what a discount is worth. Second, the monotone anchor is one vectorised line — a reversed cumulative max — “the value of standing at tier a is the best of every deeper-or-equal tier.”

The value function is built over the full 17-tier grid even though only five tiers are choosable right now: deepening to 32.5% this hour is valued knowing every deeper move that remains available after it.

STEP 5 · the action set offered now

Restrict what may be chosen, not what is known

Mid-episode, the offer is every tier at or deeper than the current anchor — here 30% through 40%, five tiers, ₩7,000 down to ₩6,000. The 25% and shallower tiers were choosable once; the ratchet has closed them. (The same solver’s other mode is the entry decision, where a fresh episode is offered five coarse arms around d_ref instead — few, well-separated prices, because that is what elasticity identification wants.)

STEP 6 · read the answer

Q over the choosable tiers

Each tier’s Q is the total expected Inventory Loss of pricing there now and playing optimally forever after — margin given away plus expected scrap. Closer to zero is better.

Bar length = size of the expected loss |Q|, in ₩. Solve time: 3.9 ms.

The suggested price is ₩7,000 — hold. At ε = −1.5 with two units left, every extra 2.5 pp of depth gives away more margin than the extra sell-through recovers, so Q worsens monotonically toward the floor. The spread is not waste, though — it is exactly what exploration prices: forcing 35% instead of the hold costs Q* − Q₃₅ = ₩452, and that is the spread the τ ledger charges when the system pays for evidence at this hour.

The closed form agrees with the hold: deepening below d pays only when |ε| > (1−d)/(γ−d) = 2.33 at d = 30% (γ = cost/price), and the posterior says 1.5. That formula is the analytic shadow of what the DP computes numerically — and it is optimistic: censoring at small inventory pushes the true switch point higher still.

Next hour the same question is asked again from whatever state remains — one unit sold and two hours left, or two units and the clock one hour shorter — and the answer can only stay or deepen.

25% is gone for good

From the full grid, 25% would score better than any choosable tier at ε = −1.5 — but an earlier hour committed to 30%, and markdown never retreats. The solver optimises given that history; it never litigates it.

Caveats the code itself states. The deepening threshold is optimistic under censoring. Mid-window restocks are not modelled — episodes with arrivals are excluded from dp_eligible rather than priced wrong. Exhaustive evaluation is affordable (≤17 tiers × ≤12 hours × ≤30 units), so nothing is approximated.
