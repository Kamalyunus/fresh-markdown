One Tag, Three Hours

How the DP solver prices a clearance episode, walked through on one worked example — every number below is real solver output. It solves the Bellman equation exactly: the state space is small enough that nothing needs approximating.

STEP 1 · feasible_tiers

Build the legal price grid

Discounts run from 0 in 2.5 pp steps up to the cost floor d_max = 1 − cost/price = 40%. That yields 17 tiers. Nothing below cost is ever representable — the floor lives in the action space itself, not in a check that could be skipped.

STEP 2 · demand at every (hour, tier)

Turn each price into a demand distribution

For every remaining hour and every tier, expected demand is the baseline scaled by the price ratio raised to the elasticity: mu = mu_ref × ((1−d)/(1−d_ref))^ε. Then a full negative-binomial distribution over sold counts is built with dispersion r — the solver never works with averages, always with the whole distribution.

STEP 3 · the terminal condition

Price the end of the window

When the clock hits zero, every unit still on the shelf is scrapped at cost: V(h=0, q) = −cost × q. Two leftover units here cost −₩12,000. This single line is where all the urgency comes from — every decision above it is a trade between giving away margin now and eating that scrap later.

STEP 4 · backward induction

Fold the future back, hour by hour

Starting from the last hour and walking backwards, the solver scores every (tier, inventory) pair:

Q[j][q] = Σₖ P(sell k | mu, r) × [ min(k, q) · (−P0·d_j) + V[t+1][j][q − min(k,q)] ] └ censoring ┘ └ margin given ┘ └ the future, anchored at j ┘ V[t][a] = max over j ≥ a of Q[j][q] ← markdown can deepen, never retreat

Two details carry the design. First, min(k, q) puts censoring inside the recursion: demand beyond stock earns nothing, so inventory level changes what a discount is worth. Second, the monotone anchor is one vectorised line — a reversed cumulative max — “the value of standing at tier a is the best of every deeper-or-equal tier.”

The value function is built over the full 17-tier grid even when the decision at hand only offers five arms: an episode entering at 15% is valued knowing it can deepen on the 2.5 pp grid every hour after.

STEP 5 · the action set offered now

Restrict what may be chosen, not what is known

At entry, only five coarse arms are offered — d_ref + {−15, −10, −5, 0, +5}pp → 15 / 20 / 25 / 30 / 35% — because elasticity identification wants few, well-separated arms. On every later hourly decision, the offer is every tier at or deeper than the current anchor: markdown monotonicity.

STEP 6 · read the answer

Q over the entry arms

Each arm’s Q is the total expected Inventory Loss of taking it now and playing optimally forever after — margin given away plus expected scrap. Closer to zero is better.

Bar length = size of the expected loss |Q|, in ₩. Solve time: 4.4 ms.

At ε = −1.5 demand is too price-insensitive to buy sell-through with margin, so the solver takes the shallowest arm. And the spread between arms is not waste — it is exactly what exploration prices: forcing the 35% arm instead of the best one costs Q* − Q₃₅ = ₩1,352, and that is the spread the τ ledger charges when the system pays for evidence.

The hourly decision anchored at 30% holds at 30%. The closed form agrees: deepening below d pays only when |ε| > (1−d)/(γ−d) = 2.33 at d = 30%, and the posterior says 1.5. That formula is the analytic shadow of what the DP computes numerically — and it is optimistic: censoring at small inventory pushes the true switch point higher.

Caveats the code itself states. The deepening threshold is optimistic under censoring. Mid-window restocks are not modelled — episodes with arrivals are excluded from dp_eligible rather than priced wrong. Exhaustive evaluation is affordable (≤17 tiers × ≤12 hours × ≤30 units), so nothing is approximated.
