The whole system, on one page

An item enters its clearance window. Every hour until it expires, the system re-reads the shelf and sets a price; when the window closes, whatever is left is scrapped at cost. The job is to give away as little as possible — in discount and in scrap together.

Three things run at three different speeds, and keeping them separate is most of the design.

Reading it in three passes

Once, before launch — the top band. Five quantities are measured from five months of history and written to files: how much a thing sells at the reference price, a level correction, how lumpy demand is, how much the hours inside one window repeat each other, and a starting guess at price response. During the MVP these are read on every decision and written by nothing.

Every hour — the middle band. The state is three numbers: units on hand, hours left, and the discount already in force. It is validated rather than repaired — an implausible state returns no price at all. Then the legal prices are constructed from the cost floor, so a below-cost price is never considered rather than considered and rejected. The DP scores every one of them exactly and takes the best. Occasionally, and within a budget, it takes a deliberately different one.

Once a day — the bottom band. Only the deliberately-randomised prices teach us anything, and only those outcomes enter the update. Evidence is discounted for the fact that hours within an episode repeat each other, the step is capped, a human approves it, and each outcome is spent exactly once. What changes is one number: the belief about price response. Which then feeds tomorrow's prices — the arrow back up to the DP is the entire product.

Three properties worth stating before anything else

Safety is structural, not checked. The action set is built from the cost floor and from the price already in force, so a below-cost price and a price rise are unrepresentable — on every path, including forced exploration. There is no validation step to forget.

The decision holds no state. The hourly path reads the shelf and the files, computes, emits a price, and forgets. Nothing is carried between hours, so there is no accumulated state to drift or to reconcile after a restart.

Exactly one thing learns. Everything else is frozen precisely so that when the belief moves, we know it moved because of evidence and not because something else shifted underneath it.

Why the system is split this way

Five months of history tells us accurately how much of a thing sells in a given hour. It cannot tell us what happens if we change the price — and not because the data is thin. Two specific confounds make that question unanswerable from records of a policy that was never randomised.

So the system is split. Everything history genuinely supports is measured once and pinned in a file. The single quantity it cannot support is given a wide, honest starting guess and then learned in production, from prices we randomise on purpose.

Frozen is a phase, not a doctrine

Freezing buys one specific thing: a fixed yardstick for the MVP and the experiment. If the demand model moved while we were measuring whether a pricing change helped, we could not say which of the two caused the difference — and the honest answer would be that we no longer know. So for this phase the rule is absolute, and every report stamps the model version it ran against so the check is mechanical rather than remembered.

Once the experiment has read out, that constraint lifts. The baseline moves to a regular retraining schedule like any other production model, and the level factors track drift between retrains. Nothing in the architecture depends on the model staying still — only the measurement does.

The one that moves

posterior.json holds the belief about price response, and it is the only thing production changes. It starts at the prior, updates only from outcomes where the price was genuinely randomised, and each update is bounded, human-gated, and consumed exactly once. Everything else here exists to make that one number learnable safely — the Learning tab is how it moves.
